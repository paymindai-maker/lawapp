## Design Context

### Users
Indian business owners, startup founders, and corporate decision-makers in India seeking premium legal representation. They browse in professional contexts — desktops during business hours, occasionally mobile. The job-to-be-done: quickly assess credibility, identify if ForLaw covers their legal need, and book a consultation. Trust signals matter enormously; this audience is skeptical of generic-looking websites.

### Brand Personality
**Bold · Decisive · Trusted** — the voice of a top-tier law firm that has been winning cases for 18 years. Not warm and approachable; confident and authoritative. Communicates with precision. Does not hedge.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Editorial magazine meets premium Indian legal practice.** Think Bloomberg Businessweek or The Economist applied to a Mumbai law firm. Light primary theme with high-contrast dark accent sections alternating through the page. Section transitions use shaped dividers (diagonal cuts, waves) — never flat edges. Typography uses Abril Fatface for display moments (commanding, editorial) and Barlow for headings/body (clean, slightly condensed, professional).

Anti-references: Generic SaaS landing pages with orb backgrounds. Law firm sites that all look the same (navy header, serif font, stock photos of gavels). Glassmorphism cards.

### Design Principles
1. **Alternating rhythm**: Light and dark sections alternate, with shaped transitions between them — never two sections of the same tone adjacent.
2. **Editorial hierarchy**: Text hierarchy must be extreme — display type 4–6× larger than body. Nothing "medium-sized."
3. **No glassmorphism, no orbs**: Background animations use geometric line patterns and floating outline shapes only.
4. **No stars on team cards**: Star ratings are inappropriate for advocates and chartered accountants.
5. **Storytelling arc**: Who we are → What we do → Why trust us. Each section advances the narrative; copy and layout reinforce this progression.
